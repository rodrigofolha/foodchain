── Variable definitions ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • k             = number of wallets that fire makeOrder concurrently via Promise.all
  • blocks/span   = unique block numbers the OK transactions landed in (across all bursts)
  • per-tx latency = wall-clock from sendTransaction to receipt for that wallet
  • per-burst wall-clock = max(per-tx) within a burst (time from first send to last receipt)
  • effective TPS = (OK tx in burst) / (burst wall-clock seconds), averaged across bursts

── Analysis ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Block time = 12000 ms, mean gas/tx ≈ 1900409, block gas limit ≈ 30,000,000.
  ⇒ Theoretical capacity ceiling: 15 makeOrders per block
     = 1.250 tx/sec sustainable.

  Look for the 'cliff' where 'blocks/span' jumps to >1 per burst — that is the point
  where k exceeds the per-block tx capacity and transactions overflow into the next
  block (adding +blockTimeMs latency).

  Above the cliff, effective TPS plateaus around (block_capacity / block_time_s).
  Below the cliff, effective TPS scales linearly with k.

  Reference points from the literature:
    • Lee 2021 (Caliper on Eth private): ~1000 tps Query, <100 tps on Ropsten testnet
    • BLOCKBENCH (Dinh 2017): Ethereum ceiling ~80 tps with 8 concurrent YCSB clients
    • Schäffer 2019: Ethereum mainnet ~15 tps
    • BCTMark (Saingre 2020): bracketed test at 5 / 50 / 200 tx/s sustained load
