# Throughput with 512 clients at 12 s blocks, redone (2026-10-19)

Local in-process Hardhat network only (Hardhat 2.28.6 with EDR, chain id 31337, block gas limit 60,000,000, main-network gas schedule, a fresh deployment per run). Nothing was sent to any network; no `.env`, key or wallet file was opened; nothing was committed or deleted. Host as in `benchmarking/results/throughput-final-2026-10-13.md` (i5-12500H, 16 threads, 15.8 GB, WSL2, Node v20.20.2). Measured 2026-10-20 00:19 to 01:12 UTC by the host clock (2026-10-19 local time), one run at a time, each after the 1-minute load average was below 0.6. Machine-readable figures: `throughput-512-2026-10-19.json`.

This note asks whether the 512-client run (run F2) can be redone to get the exact rate, that is, with no cycle failing because of the stipend.

## 1. In short

- **With the settings asked for** (the manager dApp's gas limit for the withdrawal, gas money sized for the price the level reaches, fees read for each transaction; run G512), no cycle failed for the stipend, but **31 of 512 cycles timed out** after 600 s and the rate was **0.646 cycles/s**, below the 0.762 of 256 clients. The cause is another effect of the base fee: transactions signed early kept the fee cap of that moment while the full blocks raised the base fee, were passed over by fresher transactions with a higher effective tip, and were then priced out until the base fee fell (section 4.2).
- **With, in addition, a maximum fee that no base fee of the level reaches** (`FEE_CAP_GWEI=20`, run H512), **512 of 512 cycles completed at 0.948 cycles/s, 91.7 % of the gas ceiling** (1.034 cycles/s), with 41 consecutive blocks at least 93 % full. The rate is now limited by the block gas limit.
- **The controls with 256 clients:** the settings asked for leave the rate unchanged (G256: 0.762 cycles/s, run F: 0.762). The added fee cap does not: it raises the 256-client rate to **0.853 cycles/s** (H256), because it also removes the delay of the 40 withdrawals that waited 120 to 216 s in G256 (41 in run F). Run F's 0.762 therefore includes that delay too.
- 768 clients were not run (section 6).

## 2. The cause of run F2's failures, confirmed

From run F2's raw file (`benchmarking/results/concurrent-hardhat-privacy-distributed-final-k512-12000ms-2026-10-13T03-20-06-290Z-raw.json`) and the script:

- All 211 failures are the same check of the script before sending the withdrawal (`stealthFees`, `concurrent-lib.cjs`): `poolWithdraw: stealth balance 2546694105600000 does not cover 0 + 1600000 gas x <price>`. The balance is the stipend 1,600,000 × 1.0611 gwei × 1.5 for every failed client: all 512 `makeOrder` were prepared within 12.1 to 16.7 s of the level's start, before the base fee moved, at the same `eth_gasPrice` of 1.0611 gwei.
- The prices at the failed checks were 1.739 (59 clients), 1.829 (66), 1.928 (67) and 2.042 gwei (19), 1.64 to 1.92 times the price at the order. The failed clients are those whose `makeOrder` was sent last (14.6 to 16.4 s) and therefore waited longest in the queue; they failed between 280 and 315 s.
- The withdrawal was sent with the fixed limit of 1,600,000 gas (`meta.withdrawGasLimit`). With the manager dApp's limit (the largest the script sent in run B: 1,353,434) the balance covers 1.882 gwei: 125 of the 211 would have passed the check, 86 not (computed).
- The failed clients had sent the announcement, `makeOrder` and the pool deposit (633 transactions, 443,474,213 gas).
- The type-2 transactions carried the fees read once at the level's start: 1,839 of them paid exactly 1.1500 gwei, the cap (2 × base fee + 1 gwei), and none paid more; the base fee reached 1.270 gwei. 615 transactions waited more than 120 s (485 pool deposits, 60 `confirmIntention`, 58 `makeOrder`, 12 withdrawals). The earlier file gives this cap as 1.12 gwei (section 5 of `throughput-final-2026-10-13.md`); the raw file shows 1.150 gwei.

## 3. Settings and why

Everything of run F2 is kept: one cycle per client, `PROVERS=distributed` with the emulated proving time of 755.95 ms (the same calibration file, `gas-equivalent-verifier-calibration-2026-10-13T02-31-54-926Z.json`), `DEVICE_WITNESS_CHECK=1`, 12 s blocks, `TX_TIMEOUT_MS=600000`, `MIMALLOC_PURGE_DELAY=0`, each level in a fresh process. Added, all opt-in (section 8):

| Setting | Value | Runs | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `WITHDRAW_GAS_LIMIT` | 1,392,000 | G, H | The manager dApp's limit is the node's estimate × 1.2. The script's own switch for that (`WITHDRAW_ESTIMATE=1`) runs one estimate per withdrawal on the EVM that also mines the blocks, one at a time: in a 4-client test the withdrawal's preparation took 0.83 to 1.11 s against 0.04 s without it, so 512 withdrawals would queue about 400 s on the mining thread, which a network does not do. The fixed value is the dApp's rule at the largest withdrawal of the earlier 12 s runs (1,150,948 gas × 1.2094, the ratio of the dApp's limit to the gas used in run B); the limits the dApp's rule gave in runs B and B2 ranged from 1,323,924 to 1,386,715. The largest withdrawal in these runs used 1,130,657 gas. |
| `STIPEND_FACTOR` | 10 | G, H | The stipend becomes 1,600,000 gas × the price at the order × 10 instead of × 1.5. Only the amount moved changes, not the gas of any transaction, so any value that does not bind gives the same run. Bound: the level's gas (512 × 4.84 million = 41.3 blocks of 60 million) lets the base fee rise at most 1.125^41.3 = 130 times, from 0.066 to 8.5 gwei; the price at the withdrawal is then at most 9.5 gwei, 8.96 times the 1.061 gwei of the order, and at a limit of 1,392,000 gas the factor needed is at most 7.8. Measured: at most 1.99 (G512). The dApps keep 1.5: these runs measure capacity, not the dApps' margin. |
| `FEES_PER_TX` | 1 | G, H | The customer, the establishment's main wallet and the courier send without fee fields, so ethers reads 2 × the base fee of the latest block + `eth_maxPriorityFeePerGas` (1 gwei) for each transaction, as a wallet does, instead of once per level. |
| `FEE_CAP_GWEI` | 20 | H only | Added after G512 (section 4.2). Every transaction is EIP-1559 with a maximum fee of 20 gwei and the node's 1 gwei tip; the one-time address's too, its maximum fee lowered to what its balance covers (12.2 gwei for the withdrawal at factor 10). 20 gwei is above 2 × 8.5 + 1 = 18 gwei, the ethers rule at the highest base fee the level can reach. A transaction pays base fee + tip whatever its cap, so the prices paid follow the base fee as before; what changes is that a waiting transaction keeps its full tip and is never priced out, so the transactions enter the blocks in the order they arrive. The one-time address no longer sends legacy transactions as the manager dApp does. |
| `FEE_TRACE` | 1 | G, H | Writes the level's blocks one by one (gas used, base fee) and the stipend's price on each `makeOrder` record. No effect on the run. |

### 4.1 G512: no stipend failure

The factor that the withdrawal's check needed (limit × `eth_gasPrice` when the withdrawal was prepared / (1,600,000 × the price at the order)) was at most 1.99 (median 1.00), below 10. The price at the check is read from the block rows (base fee of the next block + 1 gwei) and matches the legacy price paid by all 481 withdrawals exactly.

### 4.2 G512: why 31 cycles timed out

- The 31 transactions without a receipt are 20 `makeOrder` sent 16.3 to 16.7 s and 7 pool deposits sent 24.2 s into the level and 4 withdrawals sent at 37 and 49 s.
- The 512 `makeOrder` were signed within 12 to 17 s, at a cap of 2 × 0.066 + 1 = 1.13 gwei. Seven blocks took 46 each (322); then, from block 29 to block 43, none, although the blocks stayed 98 to 99.5 % full: the other clients' later steps, signed later, offered the full 1 gwei tip, while a waiting `makeOrder` offered only its cap minus the base fee (0.06 to 0.37 gwei when the late ones were finally included). From block 48 the base fee (1.20 gwei) was above their cap. The rest entered only when the base fee fell below it (33 in block 44, 31 in 47, 45 in 57, 43 in 62, 18 in 70); 20 did not within 600 s.
- Meanwhile the blocks emptied (blocks 49 to 82 mostly below 50 % full, the base fee oscillating between 1.0 and 1.5 gwei), because the clients behind the stalled transactions could not continue.
- With the fee cap (H512) all 512 `makeOrder` entered in blocks 22 to 33 in the order they arrived, and no transaction waited more than 142 s.

### 4.3 What limits the rate now (H512)

The block gas limit. The 41 blocks between the first (the 512 announcements, 22 % full) and the last three (the clients finishing) were all at least 93 % full; the 1 to 7 % they leave is the gas-limit rule of the protocol (a transaction enters only while the gas left covers its gas limit, 4,000,000 for `confirmIntention` and `deliveryOrder` in the script). What separates 0.948 from the ceiling is that each client runs one cycle, so the window includes a block where all clients start and blocks where they finish. The process was mostly idle (0.36 threads), blocks came every 12.0 s and memory stayed far from the limit. Every transaction paid the base fee, which the 41 full blocks raised from 0.066 to 6.3 gwei (96 times); the price per gas rose from 1.06 to 7.3 gwei.

## 5. Against runs F and F2

- **256 clients.** G256 reproduces run F (0.762 against 0.762 cycles/s, 28 blocks with 19 full, 40 against 41 slow withdrawals): the settings asked for change nothing where nothing failed. The slow withdrawals are those of the one-time addresses, which send legacy transactions priced when sent; as the base fee rises their tip falls below the full tip of fresher type-2 transactions, so they wait (the same mechanism as in section 4.2, without the timeout). With the fee cap they keep their place, the tail of the level shortens from 336 to 300 s and the rate rises to 0.853 (H256).
- **512 clients.** F2: 211 failed at the stipend check, 0.597 cycles/s. G512: no stipend failure, 31 timeouts, 0.646. H512: none failed, 0.948. From 256 to 512 clients the rate rose from 0.853 to 0.948 under the same settings (H), and the share of full blocks from 18 of 25 to 41 of 45.
- **Which figure to compare with 0.76.** 0.948 is the capacity with fees that never bind. Under the dApps' fee rules (legacy price of the one-time address, a wallet's cap of 2 × base fee + tip) the rate at 512 clients was lower than at 256 (G512, 0.646), and not every cycle completed within 600 s.

## 6. 768 clients: not run

Under the settings asked for the rate fell from 256 to 512 clients, so the condition for the optional level (a rate still rising) was not met there. Under the H settings it was still rising, but a level of 768 clients (62 blocks of gas) lets the base fee rise up to about 1.125^62 ≈ 1,500 times, to about 100 gwei, so both the stipend factor and the fee cap would have had to be raised (to about 100 and 200 gwei), which is no longer the configuration of H512. Memory would have allowed it (H512 left at least 9.9 GB available).

## 7. The dApps' margin of 1.5 (limitation)

The customer dApp sizes the one-time address's gas money for a price 1.5 times that at the order (1.72 times at the 1,392,000-gas limit used here), so a withdrawal delayed while full blocks raise the base fee can find the address unable to pay for it: the price at the check rose up to 1.92 times in run F2 (211 cycles failed) and 2.28 times in G512, where 191 of the 481 withdrawals would have failed with the dApps' margin. In H512, where the transactions entered in order, it rose at most 1.46 times and the margin of 1.5 would have covered the check of every withdrawal (computed, not run).

## 9. What these runs do not show

- What the dApps do in the cases of section 4.2: a wallet lets its user replace a stuck transaction with a higher fee; the script does not, and the manager dApp was not run.
- The base fee and prices are those of the local node, whose base fee starts far below the 1 gwei tip; on a network where the base fee is most of the price, a cap of 2 × base fee + tip binds after about six full blocks instead of about 25.
- The withdrawal limit of 1,392,000 gas is the dApp's rule at its upper end, fixed for the run, not one estimate per withdrawal.
- In H the one-time address sends EIP-1559 transactions, not the legacy ones of the manager dApp.
- Section 7's statement about H512 is computed from the run's prices with the dApps' factor, not run with it.
- Each configuration was run once.
