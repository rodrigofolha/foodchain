# Whole item prices instead of a rounded total — the order total against public menus, real menus

**The menus are real (one city, one platform, 2019); the orders are simulated.** The recommendation is modelled as a change of the registered prices; whether establishments would follow it was not studied.

## Reading (written after the run; the numbers are filled in from the JSON, except where the text says otherwise)

The figures quoted here are for orders with mostly 1–2 items and an observer who assumes at most 4 items, as in the summary of the earlier report; the tables below also give the other item-count distribution.

**In short.** If every establishment registers whole prices, the order total tells the observer as much as it does when the total is rounded up to 1 lira, and no more. With 20 establishments in a zone: 0.0% of the orders can only have come from one menu, a uniform guess among the candidates is right for 5.2% of the orders (baseline 5.0%), and a weighted guess for 9.2% (fewest items) to 18.3% (Bayes). The earlier rows are 2.1%, 12.9% and 21.5% to 33.2% for the original prices, and 0.0%, 5.2% and 9.3% to 18.5% for the total rounded up to 1 lira. The recommendation only reaches these figures if every establishment of the zone follows it. An establishment that follows it gets most of the benefit for its own orders whatever the others do; the orders of those that do not follow become easier to attribute as adoption grows.

**Full adoption, whole lira (a, b).** Rounding to the nearest lira and rounding up give the same figures to the first decimal at every zone size. E = 5: unique 0.1%, uniform guess 20.5% (baseline 20.0%), fewest items 30.5%, Bayes 44.2%, against 13.0%, 36.4%, 49.6% and 61.6% with the original prices. E = 50: 0.0%, 2.1% (baseline 2.0%), 4.0% and 9.5%, against 0.2%, 5.6%, 10.8% and 19.6%. The mean number of candidate establishments per order goes from 13.78 to 19.61 of 20 (from 3.66 to 4.92 of 5, from 34.48 to 49.01 of 50): nearly every menu can form nearly every whole total.

**Partial adoption (c).** Averaged over all the orders of a zone, partial adoption changes little: at E = 20 the uniform guess is 13.0%, 12.2% and 9.8% with 25%, 50% and 75% of the establishments following (12.9% with none), Bayes 32.1%, 29.4% and 24.3% (33.2% with none), and the share of orders with a single candidate rises (2.9%, 3.1%, 3.2%, from 2.1%). The average hides two groups. The orders of the followers: at 25% adoption, unique 0.0%, uniform guess 5.8%, fewest items 12.1%, Bayes 23.9%, with 17.53 candidates on average; at 75%, 0.0%, 5.3%, 10.1% and 19.7%. So a follower gains even when three quarters of the zone do not follow: the certain identification and most of the uniform-guess advantage are gone at once, and the Bayes figure goes more than half of the way to its value under full adoption. The reason is in the data: most of the non-followers' menus contain whole prices (median share of whole prices in a menu 83.9%) and can form a whole total, so they remain candidates for a follower's order. The orders of the others: uniform guess 15.3%, 18.6% and 22.9%, unique 3.9%, 6.2% and 12.6%, Bayes 34.8%, 37.4% and 38.1%. A total with cents can only come from a menu with cents, and there are fewer of those as adoption grows. The same pattern holds at E = 5, at E = 50 (followers at 25%: uniform guess 2.3%, Bayes 13.2%; the others at 75%: 13.7% and 27.6%) and with all 659 restaurants. Two more follow seeds (2 and 3, separate runs whose numbers are not in the JSON of this report) gave, at E = 20 and 25% adoption, 5.7% and 5.8% (uniform guess) and 23.7% and 24.6% (Bayes) for the followers, and 14.8% and 14.7%, 34.1% and 33.5% for the others.

**Multiples of 5 lira (d).** The stronger recommendation leaves unique and the uniform guess where whole prices put them (0.0% and 5.1% at E = 20) and lowers the weighted guesses: fewest items 6.4%, Bayes 11.5% (nearest multiple, at least 5), 12.2% (a price below 2.50 becomes 1) and 11.7% (rounded up), against 18.3% with whole prices. This is the level of the earlier row "total rounded up to 5" (6.6% and 12.7%). At E = 50 Bayes is 5.3% against 9.5%; at E = 5, 33.8% against 44.2%.

**What the observer still has.** With whole prices almost every menu of the zone is a candidate for almost every order, so what is left is not exclusion but weighting: a total of 85 is more likely to come from a menu of dear dishes than from a menu where it takes four items. The price level of an establishment and the spread of its prices are untouched by rounding to a unit that is small against the prices (median item 21.50 lira). That this is what remains is argued, not measured separately; what is measured is that the Bayes guess stays at 3.7× the baseline with whole prices and at 2.3× with multiples of 5 at E = 20 (4.8× and 2.7× at E = 50), and the guess that needs no model (fewest items) at 1.8× and 1.3×. The Bayes figure assumes an observer who knows how customers order, and is an upper bound for this workload. The observer also keeps everything outside the total (delivery fee, timing, the customer's address), which this measurement does not cover.

**How artificial the change is.** 61.3% of the 57,761 item prices are already whole and 273 of the 659 restaurants have at least 90% of their items at whole prices, but only 78 restaurants have nothing to change; the typical cents are .50, .99 and .90, and the convention is the restaurant's (.99 or .90 for 124 of them). 93 items cost less than 0.50 lira and would round to zero at the nearest lira; they are kept at 1 lira. Only 18.4% of the prices are multiples of 5 lira, and 1,377 items cost less than 2.50 lira and would round to zero at the nearest multiple of 5.

**Cost.** Whole lira, nearest: 22,379 item prices change, by 1.57% of the price on average over all items (0.09 lira; 0.34% of the sum of the prices), almost always upwards; an order total changes by 0.48% on average (0.13 lira), and 37.2% of the orders change at all. Rounding up costs the same (1.60% and 0.49%). Multiples of 5 are another matter: 16.79% per item and 5.97% per order total at the nearest multiple with a floor of 5 (the cheapest item, at 0.20 lira, becomes 5.00), 9.66% and 5.43% if the cheap items go to 1 lira, 21.40% and 11.37% rounded up. For that price the gain over whole lira is in the weighted guesses only.

## Method

**Data.** Kilic, O. and Taşkaya Temizel, T., "Pita and pizza restaurants and menus in Ankara" (dataset). Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.10554680 (https://zenodo.org/records/10554680), licence CC BY 4.0 (Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International). File `Data/Original/yemeksepeti_restaurants_ankara-pizza_pide-info_20191002.json` (10,386,923 bytes, sha256 115c2808ce83e115cf544e28d56a400477b8613b42a3fee7a8e900b1c493a4df), the copy downloaded on 2026-10-11 for the earlier run (archive `Kilic_Temizel_Pita_Pizza.zip`, md5 1e22795fced5e47fd3dd4eae86ec3ac0, checked again). 659 restaurants, 57,761 priced items in Turkish lira (6 items with a price ≤ 0 dropped). 1 lira is treated as 1 currency unit of the dApp; a "unit" below is one lira. The dataset is not in the repository.

**Attack, orders and observer: unchanged.** The code path of the earlier run is used as it is (the units of work are its `real-sample` and `real-all` units with other menus, the random streams have the same labels). An order: establishment uniform, mostly 1–2 items (0.45/0.35/0.15/0.05) or uniform on 1–4 items (0.25/0.25/0.25/0.25), items uniform and independent, redrawn while the total is below 5.00. The orders are drawn on the menus of the variant (the customer pays the registered prices). The observer knows every registered menu of the zone and the order model; measures, all with at most 4 items (K = 4, the figures quoted from the earlier report; K = 6 is in the JSON):

- **Unique**: share of the orders whose total only one menu of the zone can form.
- **Uniform guess**: probability that a guess drawn uniformly among the menus that can form the total is right (called "forced" in the earlier report). Without the total it is the baseline, 1 / number of establishments of the zone.
- **Fewest items**: a guess for the menu that forms the total with the fewest items (ties split).
- **Bayes**: a guess for the menu most likely to produce the total under the order model, which the observer is assumed to know exactly.
- **Candidates**: number of menus of the zone that can form the total, mean (median).

**Zones.** Each of 100 seeds draws 50 restaurants without replacement; the first 5 and 20 give E = 5 and E = 20, "20 in 4 zones" as before; 20,000 orders per seed, item-count distribution and layout. "All 659" puts every restaurant in one zone or in its district (14 zones): 5 seeds × 20,000 orders (only the orders change between seeds).

**Variants of the menus.**

| Variant | Rule |
|---|---|
| `full` | original prices (the earlier run's "real, as published") |
| `near1` | (a) every price rounded to the nearest whole lira (the earlier run's "real, prices rounded to whole units") |
| `up1` | (b) every price rounded up to a whole lira |
| `near1-f25` | (c) 25% of the establishments round to the nearest whole lira |
| `near1-f50` | (c) 50% of the establishments round to the nearest whole lira |
| `near1-f75` | (c) 75% of the establishments round to the nearest whole lira |
| `near5` | (d) every price at the nearest multiple of 5 lira, at least 5 |
| `near5min1` | (d) every price at the nearest multiple of 5 lira; below 2.50 it becomes 1 |
| `up5` | (d) every price rounded up to a multiple of 5 lira |

"Nearest" sends a price half way between two multiples up (x.50 becomes x + 1). A price that a rule would bring to zero is kept: at 1 lira for the whole-lira rules, and at 5 lira or at 1 lira (two variants) for the multiples of 5. In the partial variants (c) the followers are the first 25%, 50% and 75% of one random order of the 659 restaurants (follow seed 1; 165 at 25%, 330 at 50%, 494 at 75%; the sets are nested), the others keep their published prices; a zone of a seed then holds a random number of followers.

**Rows of the earlier report.** "Original prices" with the exact total, and with the total rounded up to 1 or 5 lira, are recomputed here by the same code and seeds and compared with the earlier JSON (`menu-fingerprint-2026-10-11.json.gz`): 75,504 values of 72 configurations (`full` against its `full`, `near1` against its `whole`: means, percentiles, per-seed values, histograms), all equal.

## Limitations

- **Everything said in the earlier report holds here**: one city, one platform, pizza and pide restaurants, one day in 2019, lira; simulated orders (uniform establishments and items, 1–4 items, a minimum of 5.00); the Bayes column assumes the observer knows the order model exactly and is an upper bound for this workload; only the total is used (not the delivery fee, the ciphertext length, timing or the customer's address); menus are assumed known and current.
- **A recommendation is not a rule.** The variants assume that an establishment that follows it changes every item. An establishment that keeps a single item at x.50 or x.99 produces non-whole totals for the orders that contain it. The partial variants model whole establishments that do not follow, not partial menus.
- **The follower's gain under partial adoption depends on the other menus.** Here most menus that keep their prices still contain whole prices and stay candidates for a whole total. In a market where the others price everything at x.90 or x.99, a whole total would point at the few followers; that case is not in this dataset and was not simulated.
- **Who follows is drawn at random.** In practice the establishments that already price in whole units (most of this dataset) follow at no cost and the ones with a .90 or .99 convention are the ones asked to change; adoption correlated with the current convention was not simulated. The (c) figures are for one follow seed.
- **Lira of 2019 as the unit.** How artificial the change is depends on the price level in units: here the median item costs 21.50 units. In a currency where a dish costs 8 to 12 units, a whole-unit grid is a coarser change (the synthetic part of the earlier report used a median of 8.80) and multiples of 5 are not plausible.
- **Cost is a price change only.** No demand response, no menu psychology (the .90 and .99 endings exist for a reason), no tax or accounting constraint. The order-cost baskets are drawn on the original menus; the attack orders are drawn on the variant menus.
- **Nothing was implemented** in the dApp or the contracts; the dApp does not enforce or suggest whole prices today.

## Layout of the JSON

- `meta`: date, Node version, seeds, orders, follow seed, parameters, sha256 of the script. `source`, `file`: the dataset. `profile`: the profile of the earlier report; `priceProfile`: endings and whole prices.
- `rules`, `variants`, `followers` (number of followers per share), `layouts`.
- `configs[]`: one record per `variant` × `layout` (E5, E20, E50, E20z4, all1, allDistricts) × `itemCount` × `rounding` of the on-chain total in cents (0, 100, 500), with the fields of the earlier JSON (`baseline`; `unique4`, `forced4`, `meanC4`, the same with 6, `fewest`, `bayes`, each with mean, sd, se, min, max, p10, p90 and per-seed values; histograms; order values). The partial variants also have `groups[]` (index 1 = follows, 0 = does not): `orders`, the metrics pooled over the orders of the group, and `overSeeds`.
- `itemCost`, `orderCost`: the cost tables. `comparison`, `crosscheck`: the validation results quoted above.

## Reproduce

```bash
F=<path>/yemeksepeti_restaurants_ankara-pizza_pide-info_20191002.json   # from the Zenodo archive, see benchmarking/privacy-eval/menu-fingerprint/README.md
cd benchmarking/privacy-eval/menu-fingerprint
node menu-fingerprint.cjs --integer-prices --real $F --dump-integer-crosscheck
python3 -I crosscheck_integer_prices.py crosscheck/integer-prices-cases.json $F
node menu-fingerprint.cjs --integer-prices --real $F --seeds 100 --orders 20000 --seeds-all 5 --follow-seed 1 --cost-orders 400000 --jobs 8 --compare-with ../../results/menu-fingerprint-2026-10-11.json.gz --out <dir>   # 57 s here
```

The values given to `--seeds`, `--orders`, `--seeds-all`, `--follow-seed` and `--cost-orders` are the defaults. Seeds 1 to N are used; the random streams are labelled by seed, layout and item-count distribution, so a run with the same options gives the same numbers.
