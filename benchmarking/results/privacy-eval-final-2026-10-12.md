## Summary (written after the runs; every measured number is in the tables below or in the JSON, and was computed again by script)

Where a figure is followed by two figures in brackets, they are **the minimum and the maximum of three seeds**, not a confidence interval. Tables 1a, 1b, 3, 4 and 5b come from one seed each and say so.

**How to read a linkage figure.** A percentage of orders linked to their establishment says what an observer learns only next to what it could guess without attacking any order. Three such guesses are given:
- *Uniform guess*: one of the establishments of the zone at random. Any observer, at any time.
- *Busiest wallet over the whole run*: the observer adds up what each establishment's wallet received on chain (public data) and names the establishment with the largest sum for every order. Only the observer that knows which wallet belongs to which establishment can make it (the dApps do not publish the wallets), and only after the run, since the sum is over the whole run. The attacks are made after the run too, so this is the like-for-like baseline for that observer. An order with no guess counts as wrong.
- *Busiest wallet so far when the order is made*: the same sum over the blocks before the order only. The same observer can make it at the moment the order appears; early orders have no guess and count as wrong, and the share of orders with a guess is given with it.

The observer that does not know the wallets can name a wallet in the same way, not an establishment; that variant was not computed. No figure below is the excess of a rule over a baseline unless the sentence says so.

Metrics. *precision* = correct / answered; *recall* = correct / all items (abstentions count as wrong); *forced* = expected accuracy when every abstention becomes a uniform guess among the candidates the observer cannot exclude. Percentages are over orders. A recall below a baseline only says that the observer abstained on many orders: the figure to compare with a baseline is the forced guess, the precision, or the recall of a rule that answers every order.

Baselines: guesses that use no attack on a single order. Every linkage figure of a cash-out has to be read next to them.

- *Uniform guess*: the expected accuracy of a uniform guess among the establishments of the order's zone. Any observer can make it at any time; it needs the public listing only.
- *Busiest wallet over the whole run*: for each establishment's wallet, the sum of what it received (pool withdrawals paid to it, transfers and internal transfers into it: public chain data); the establishment with the largest sum is named for every order of its zone. **Who can make it and when**: only the observer that knows which wallet belongs to which establishment (the dApps do not publish the wallets; the listing of the model carries them for that observer alone), and only after the run, because the sum is over the whole run. It uses no truth about the orders. The figure is correct / all orders; an order with no guess would count as wrong (in this grid every order has a guess whenever a wallet received anything).
- *Busiest wallet so far when the order is made*: the same sum over the blocks before the order's `makeOrder` only, so the same observer can make it at the moment the order appears, without hindsight. Before any wallet has received anything there is no guess. Three figures: correct / all orders (an order with no guess counts as wrong); the share of orders that have a guess; and the accuracy when the orders without a guess get a uniform guess instead.
- The observer that does not know the wallets can add up the same sums for the wallets it finds, but its answer is a wallet (an unnamed group of orders), not an establishment; that variant was not computed.
- *Most frequent establishment by true order counts*: the share of the orders that the establishment with the most orders has. No observer has these counts; no guess of one establishment for every order can be right more often on that order stream.

The attacks themselves are made after the run as well (a timing rule looks at the withdrawals that follow a payment), so the whole-run guess is the like-for-like baseline of the wallets-known observer; the guess made when the order appears is the stricter one.

Seeds and brackets. Where a cell reads `mean [a–b]`, a and b are **the minimum and the maximum of three seeds**, not a confidence interval. Tables made from one run say "one seed" in their title.

Two observers. *Wallets known*: the observer knows which wallet belongs to which establishment (the worst case, and the earlier report's observer). *Wallets unknown*: it does not; it finds the wallets that deposit, cash out or fund order accounts, and its answer is a wallet. *Grouped* = the order is put in the group of its establishment's wallet (an unnamed group; baseline: uniform among the wallets found). *Named* = the establishment is named, by the order's content or because other orders of its group name it. *One known order* = named when the observer also knows the establishment of one order per establishment (for example its own purchase), scored on the other orders.

Float. With the pool, each establishment's main wallet first deposits a float that funds the collateral of its orders: ceil((2 × N / E + 20) × 20) USD in 3 deposits unless a table says otherwise, which is 4,400 USD at E = 5, 1,400 USD at E = 20 and 800 USD at E = 50 for N = 500, and 800 USD at E = 20 for N = 200. Section 4c repeats the dApp's cash-out with small floats.

What the runs measure: withdrawals, merges and gas per order (cash-out gas is the main wallet's withdrawals and merges, as in table 4; top-ups are counted apart).
