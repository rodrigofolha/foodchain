## Method

**Channel** (as the code reads in the repository's working tree on 2026-10-11). `Chain.makeOrder(_restaurant, _deliveryPrice, _total, …)` carries the item total as its own argument, separate from the delivery fee. The customer dApp computes it as `priceUtils.dollarsToWei(total − deliveryFee)` with `dollarsToWei(x) = round(100·x) · 10^13` (1 USD = 10^15 wei), and the basket total is the sum of the menu prices of the items (quantities repeat the unit price). So `_total / 10^13` is the exact sum in cents. Menus with prices are public (the API route `GET /customer/restaurants/:id/menu` has no authentication and returns the items with their prices and the establishment's city, which the dApp uses as the zone), and the zone of each order is public (`_zone` in the calldata and the `OrderZone` event).

**Attack.** For an order with total T in zone z, the candidates are the establishments of z whose menu can form exactly T with at most K items, repetition allowed. K = 4 is the true maximum of the workload; K = 6 is an observer who does not know it. Sums are computed exactly in integer cents (bitsets of the sums of exactly k prices, k = 1…6). The true establishment is always a candidate (checked for every order).

**Metrics**, per configuration:
- *unique* (single candidate): share of orders with exactly one candidate. The establishment is identified with certainty (an attack that answers only then has precision 100% and this recall);
- *forced*: expected accuracy of a uniform guess among the candidates, mean of 1/|candidates| (the "forced" metric of `benchmarking/privacy-eval`);
- *candidates*: mean and median size of the candidate set (median over all orders of all seeds);
- *baseline*: uniform guess among the establishments of the order's zone, 1/E with one zone;
- two further metrics, added because the uniform guess ignores how likely each candidate is and so understates the leak when menus are large: *fewest items* = guess the candidate that forms the total with the fewest items (ties split evenly; needs no knowledge of ordering behaviour), and *Bayes* = guess the candidate with the highest likelihood of the total, for an observer who knows the item-count distribution and that items are picked uniformly (the best possible guess from the total alone under this model, so an upper bound for this workload). The likelihood counts sums of at most 4 items, the true maximum; the fewest-items guess is the same for K = 4 and K = 6.

**Establishments and zones.** E = 5, 20, 50 in one zone, and 20 over 4 zones (zone = e mod 4, 5 per zone, as `privacy-eval/workload.cjs`). The E = 5 and E = 20 sets are the first establishments of the E = 50 set of the same seed, and the 4-zone configuration uses the same 20 menus as E = 20.

**Orders.** Establishment uniform; number of items n from one of two distributions: *mostly 1–2 items* P(n = 1…4) = 0.45 / 0.35 / 0.15 / 0.05, or *uniform on 1–4 items* (0.25 / 0.25 / 0.25 / 0.25, the item count `privacy-eval` draws); each item uniform over the menu, with repetition; total = sum of the prices. A basket below 5.00 USD is redrawn with the same number of items, so the item-count distribution stays the stated one (`privacy-eval` also redraws order values below 5 USD, and the dApp refuses an order that does not cover the gas stipend); the share of baskets redrawn is in the table below. 20,000 orders per configuration and seed, 100 seeds (1–100), so 2,000,000 orders per configuration. Tables give the mean over seeds, and where shown [10th–90th percentile over seeds]; each seed is a different set of menus, so the range says how much the result depends on which menus happen to share a zone.

**Synthetic menus.** Menu sizes M = 10, 30, 60 (a menu of M items is the first M of the establishment's 60). Continuous price of an item: lognormal, median = 8.80 USD × establishment level, σ = 0.8 (log scale) inside a menu; establishment level lognormal with median 1 and σ = 0.35; redrawn outside [0.50, 150.00] USD. The two σ are the values measured on the real dataset (within-menu SD of log price, median over restaurants 0.84; SD of the log median price between restaurants 0.34), rounded; the median was set so that the median order value with 1–4 items (uniform) is 25 USD, the median of `privacy-eval` (`--calibrate`; realised values in the table below). The same continuous prices are used by every price model; a model only decides how they become a price in cents:

- `cents` — Prices in cents, arbitrary endings (continuous price rounded to the cent).
- `retail-iid` — Retail endings .00/.50/.90/.95/.99 drawn independently per item; weights 62.41/14.50/9.71/0.01/13.37% (pooled item shares measured on the real dataset).
- `retail-house` — Retail endings with one pricing convention per establishment (share of establishments by dominant ending and the ending mix inside each group, both measured on the real dataset).
- `retail-flat` — Retail endings drawn independently per item with flatter weights .00 30%, .50 15%, .90 20%, .95 10%, .99 25% (assumption, no data behind it).
- `grid50` — Prices on a 0.50 grid (continuous price rounded to the nearest 0.50).
- `whole` — Prices in whole currency units (continuous price rounded to the nearest unit, at least 1) — also mitigation (a).

For the retail models the price is the nearest price with the drawn ending. In `retail-house` an establishment first draws its group (dominant ending .00 / .99 / .90 / .50 with probability 76.3% / 10.4% / 8.5% / 4.9%), then each item draws its ending from the group's mix (.00 group: .00 85.1%, .50 13.7%, .90 1.2%, .95 0.0%, .99 0.0%; .99 group: .00 3.2%, .50 0.5%, .90 3.5%, .95 0.0%, .99 92.9%; .90 group: .00 9.4%, .50 7.6%, .90 82.9%, .95 0.0%, .99 0.0%; .50 group: .00 27.5%, .50 69.7%, .90 2.7%, .95 0.1%, .99 0.0%).

**Mitigations.** (a) every establishment prices in whole units: the `whole` model. (b) the on-chain total is rounded up to the next multiple of 1 USD or 5 USD: the observer sees T′ = ⌈T/r⌉·r and the candidates are the establishments that can form some total in (T′ − r, T′] (and at least the minimum order) with at most K items; the customer overpays T′ − T.

**Random numbers.** xmur3 string hash → sfc32, one stream per label (`menu-fp/v1/menu/<seed>/<establishment>`, `menu-fp/v1/orders/<seed>/M<size>/<layout>/<item-count>`). The order stream does not depend on the price model, so models are compared on the same random draws up to the redraws.

## Limitations

- **Synthetic menus.** Price levels, their spread, the endings and their weights are model choices. Two of the retail models take their weights from one dataset (Ankara, 2019, lira); `retail-flat` has no data behind it. Other markets may have other conventions (.95, one of the five endings requested, is almost absent from this dataset), and results move a lot between models, which is the point of the analysis. The price level and its spread (inside a menu and between establishments) were not varied; the weighted guesses on coarse price grids depend on that spread.
- **Menu sizes.** 10, 30 and 60 items were requested. The real menus here have a median of 70 items and many repeated prices, which the lognormal model does not reproduce (a synthetic `cents` menu has no repeated price). Synthetic menus also have no sizes, extras or combos with their own price lists.
- **Ordering behaviour is simulated everywhere, including on the real menus.** Both item-count distributions are assumptions. Establishments are chosen uniformly and items are picked uniformly and independently; real orders concentrate on popular establishments, on a few popular dishes and on typical combinations (a main and a drink), have other basket sizes (more than 4 items exist), and depend on the establishment. An order with more items than the observer's K would drop the true establishment from the candidates; that case is not modelled (K ≥ 4 = the true maximum here).
- **The Bayes column assumes the observer knows the model exactly** (item-count distribution, uniform choice, minimum order). It is an upper bound for this workload, not something a real observer reaches without knowing how customers order. The fewest-items column needs none of that. With popularity data (for example best-seller lists, which platforms publish) a real observer could do better or worse than either.
- **The total only.** No other field is used: not the delivery fee (public in the same calldata, `round(1 + km)`, so it encodes the customer–establishment distance), not the ciphertext length of the item list, not timing, not the later cash-outs measured in `benchmarking/privacy-eval`, and not the customer's address (the sender of `makeOrder`), which would let an observer combine the evidence of a customer's repeated orders. Evidence is not combined across orders of the same establishment either (each order has a fresh stealth address). These channels would add to this one.
- **Menus are assumed known and current**, all establishments of the zone listed, prices in cents without rounding at checkout (true for the dApp, checked in "Validation"), no discounts, tips, service fees or minimum-order surcharges inside `_total` (the dApp has none today: `serviceFee = 0`). A price change between the observer's menu snapshot and the order can remove the true establishment from the candidates.
- **Zones.** One zone or four equal zones with uniformly chosen establishments. Real zones are cities with hundreds or thousands of establishments of very different popularity; the "all 659" row is the closest case and it still covers only the pizza and pide restaurants of one city.
- **Mitigations are evaluated against this attack only.** Rounding the total changes what the establishment receives (the surplus has to go somewhere; refunding it on-chain would reveal the exact total again), and whole-unit pricing is a rule every establishment has to follow. The residual accuracy under 5 USD rounding comes from the price level of the establishment, which rounding does not hide. Neither was implemented in the contracts or the dApp.
- **Minimum order** of 5 USD is applied to every establishment and known to the observer. The dApp has no minimum besides the gas stipend check (`gasStipend ≤ _total`).
- **Real data.** One city, one platform, two cuisine families (restaurants that sell pizza or pide), one day in 2019, prices in lira; 1 lira is treated as 1 unit of the dApp. The restaurants' own minimum basket values (in the dataset) are not used. Chain branches with identical menus are kept (they are separate establishments in the dApp). How the dataset was collected was not verified. Transcription or collection errors in the prices were not checked beyond dropping prices ≤ 0.

## Layout of the JSON

- `meta`: date, Node version, seeds, orders per seed, every parameter (`parameters`, `priceModels`), sha256 of the script.
- `synthetic.configs[]`: one record per price model (`model`) × menu size (`M`) × establishments (`layout`: E5, E20, E50, E20z4) × item-count distribution (`itemCount`) × rounding step in cents (`rounding`: 0, 100, 500). Fields: `baseline`; `unique4`, `forced4`, `meanC4` and the same with 6 (observer's K); `fewest`; `bayes` — each with `mean`, `sd`, `se`, `min`, `max`, `p10`, `p90` over seeds and the per-seed values; `medianC4`, `medianC6` and the candidate-set size histograms `hist4`, `hist6` (orders of all seeds); `overpayCents`, `meanTotalCents`, `medianTotalCents`, `redrawRate` (summaries over seeds); `nShare` (realised shares of 1–4 items).
- `synthetic.menuStats[]`: per price model and menu size, mean number of distinct prices, mean item price (cents), mean change of a price when rounded to whole units, and how many menus have which greatest common divisor of their prices.
- `real`: `source`, `file` (size, sha256), `profile` (what was measured on the menus), `constantsCheck`, `wholePriceChange`, and `configs[]` with the same fields as above, keyed by `variant` (full, sub60, sub30, sub10, whole) and `layout` (also all1, allDistricts).
- `selfcheck`, `crosscheck`: the validation results quoted above.
