Mean precision 98.6–100% (lowest seed 95.9%, E = 50; the wrong answers are timing guesses on unmatched cash-outs), mean recall 80–96%; among orders whose revenue had left the pool, 98.1–98.6%. The rest of the recall gap is revenue still in the pool when the run ended (8.5% of revenue at E = 20).
   - Larger batches stop the exact attack: S2:10 and S2:20 had no match. The expected number of chance solutions per cash-out is 10^5.1 and 10^20.9, so even an exhaustive search could not pick the true batch. The price is revenue held back: 16.7% and 25.3% of revenue was still in the pool at the end (about 25 orders per establishment in these runs, so this share depends on the run length).
   - The wei-level digits come from the gas stipend. With a fixed gas price (amounts on a cents grid) S1 is still 100% linked, but all 87 S2:5 cash-outs were ambiguous (median 10^4.1 chance solutions) and P1 fell to 8.8% recall.

## 4. Cash-out strategies: linkage and cost (FULL, E = 20, N = 500, seed 1)

S1 each revenue note withdrawn as it arrives; S2:X the accumulated revenue every X notes; S3:D fixed denominations of D USD (several: largest first) after every revenue note; S3:D:pX the same, checked after every X notes. Amount attack: when the recipient sent j pure merges since its previous cash-out, the withdrawal spends j + 2 notes and that subset size is searched first (S2: the batch size, unless a note that arrived during the merge chain was merged in); without a solution of that size, sizes 1–6. "Unmatched, unique expected" (S1/S2 only): of the cash-outs with such a hint that the observer did not match, those for which an exhaustive search of that size would still have a single solution (log10 of the expected number of chance solutions on the amounts' lattice < 0; median given): there the limit is this observer's search budget (sizes <= 6, 3·10^6 partial subsets), not the information on-chain. Above 0, false solutions outnumber the true one. n/a for S3: a denomination is not a sum of whole revenue notes.
